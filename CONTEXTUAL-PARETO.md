# Feasible budgets under cost simulation

For each move of a concrete transition system require a move of the abstract system with no larger charge and a related successor. Require the same for terminal charges. Induction on the concrete path builds an abstract witness below its cost. A reverse simulation on the same relation lifts every abstract path within its budget to the concrete system. The two feasible upper sets of cost vectors agree at every finite horizon. With finite menus the minimal antichains agree, including unsupported optima. Actions can have different names and dominated duplicates can be removed. Trace recovery uses the stored local matching witness at each step. For stochastic models different distributional conditions are necessary. [Related model minimization by Dean and Givan](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1997/AAAI97-017.pdf)

For one-time setup charges, retain only the intersection of already purchased resources with the union of all possible future requests. A present request incurs the same charge after this projection. A shrinking future support gives the same projected successor. These two identities certify a reduction before any weights are chosen. Unknown future requests, eviction charges for discarding live data, and obligations requiring a discarded resource must be incorporated into the state. In particular a prefetch with higher local cost may avoid an expensive later action.

For the cost pairs (0,4), (2,3), (4,0), the middle pair satisfies the budget (2,3) alone. It cannot minimize a scalarization with strictly positive weights. The verified source also contains a current-observation counterexample to preservation of future affordability. The script enumerates all 1296 four-stage instances over six fixed binary menus and cross-checks the projected recurrence against every complete path.
